Most people assume relationships fail because of conflict — too many arguments, too many unresolved issues. The research says otherwise. According to Dr. John Gottman's decades of relationship research, the single strongest predictor that a relationship or marriage will fail isn't conflict at all. It's **contempt**.

## What Contempt Actually Looks Like

Contempt is different from anger or frustration. It's a sense of superiority over your partner — sarcasm, eye-rolling, mockery, or a tone that communicates disrespect rather than disagreement. Gottman's research identified contempt as one of what he calls the "Four Horsemen" of relationship breakdown, and of the four, it's the one most strongly linked to divorce.

## Why Contempt Is So Damaging

Healthy relationships are built on **adoration and admiration** — a genuine sense that you're impressed by, and grateful for, your partner. We choose the people we're close to because something in them feels special to us, and part of what makes a relationship feel secure is knowing we were chosen back the same way.

Contempt directly attacks that foundation. When disrespect enters a relationship, it creates a confusing, destabilizing question: *how can I be someone special in this relationship, while also being looked down on by the very person who chose me?* That contradiction undermines a person's sense of safety and belonging within the relationship — often more than any single argument could.

## Why This Matters for Couples Therapy

Understanding contempt as a specific, identifiable pattern — rather than just "we argue a lot" — changes how a relationship can be repaired. In couples therapy, the goal isn't simply reducing conflict. It's identifying whether contempt has taken root, understanding where it came from, and rebuilding the respect and admiration that relationships depend on to feel secure.

This is exactly the kind of pattern-recognition that **Gottman Method couples therapy** is built around. Rather than treating every disagreement as its own isolated problem, Gottman-trained therapists look for the underlying relational patterns — contempt, criticism, defensiveness, and stonewalling — that predict where a relationship is heading, and work directly on repairing those patterns rather than just managing individual conflicts as they arise.

## If This Sounds Familiar

If you've noticed contempt creeping into your relationship — sarcasm that's replaced real conversation, a tone of disrespect that wasn't always there — that's not a sign the relationship is beyond repair. It's a sign worth paying attention to, and one of the most treatable patterns in couples therapy when addressed directly.
