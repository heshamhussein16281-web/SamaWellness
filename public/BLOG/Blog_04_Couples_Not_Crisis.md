Couples therapy is often thought of as a last resort — something you turn to only when a relationship is already falling apart, right before separation or divorce becomes the alternative. But that framing misses something important: relationships aren't things you find already complete. They're things you build, continuously.

## The Flower Analogy

Imagine someone gives their partner flowers, and the partner reacts with real joy — genuinely touched by the gesture. Then, nothing follows. No more effort, no repetition of the thing that created that moment of connection in the first place. It wasn't really the flowers that mattered — it was the effort behind them.

The same principle applies to relationships generally. We put in real effort at the beginning — courting, noticing, prioritizing — and then, once things feel stable, that effort often quietly stops. That's the opposite of the idea that **relationships are built, not found**. A relationship isn't a finished object you discover already perfect. It's something actively constructed, maintained, and developed over time.

## Why "Waiting for a Crisis" Doesn't Serve Relationships Well

If couples therapy is only considered once a relationship is in serious trouble, that means the relationship has typically been operating without any real maintenance for a long time before help is finally sought — which makes repair harder than it needed to be.

Couples therapy works best as exactly that: **maintenance**. Not a last resort before divorce, but a tool for:
- Strengthening the emotional connection between partners
- Deepening understanding of each other's needs, before resentment builds
- Addressing small patterns before they calcify into larger ones
- Keeping a relationship actively growing, rather than passively coasting

## What This Looks Like in Practice

Couples who come to therapy proactively — not in the middle of a crisis — often find the sessions genuinely different in tone. Instead of urgent repair work, sessions can focus on deepening connection, understanding each partner's attachment patterns, and building tools that keep the relationship strong well before any serious rupture occurs.

This is especially true with **Gottman Method couples therapy**, which is built around ongoing relationship health — not just crisis intervention. The framework gives couples concrete tools for maintaining connection, admiration, and respect as an ongoing practice, not a one-time fix.
